ISGS 101 · Islamic Studies 1: ʿAqīdah

1. Tawḥīd (M1, M3, M4)
  • Rubūbiyyah: Allah alone creates, owns, provides, controls
  • Ulūhiyyah / ʿIbādah: all worship for Allah alone (51:56)
  • Asmāʾ waṣ-Ṣifāt: affirm names/attributes with no taḥrīf, taʿṭīl, takyīf, tamthīl (42:11)
2. Lā ilāha illā Allāh: 7 conditions (M2–3)
  • ʿIlm (knowledge) · Yaqīn (certainty)
  • Qabūl (acceptance) · Inqiyād (submission)
  • Ṣidq (truthfulness) · Ikhlāṣ (sincerity)
  • Maḥabbah (love)
Īmān6 pillars (Hadith of Jibrīl)
3. Angels · Books · Messengers (M6–10)
  • Angels made of light; believe in existence, names, attributes, deeds
  • Books: Ṣuḥuf (Ibrāhīm), Tawrāh (Mūsā), Zabūr (Dāwūd), Injīl (ʿĪsā), Qur'an
  • Nabī vs Rasūl; Muḥammad ﷺ the last; prophets are human and protected from error in conveying the message
4. Last Day & Qadar (M11–12)
  • Death → grave → resurrection → gathering → intercession → reckoning → scale → bridge → Paradise/Hell
  • Qadar 4 levels: ʿIlm, Kitābah, Mashīʾah, Khalq
  • Humans still choose and are accountable
5. Shirk (M13–15)
  • Major: takes out of Islam, not forgiven without repentance (duʿāʾ to the dead, sacrifice to other than Allah)
  • Minor: riyāʾ, swearing by other than Allah, "what Allah and you will"
  • Fortune-tellers and astrology are forbidden
6. Worship (M4–5)
  • ʿIbādah = all that Allah loves, sayings and deeds, outward and inward
  • Heart: love, fear, hope, tawakkul
  • Accepted only with ikhlāṣ and following the Sunnah
  • Iḥsān: worship as if you see Him

Six pillars of Īmān

  • Belief in Allah, Angels, Books, Messengers, the Last Day, and Qadar (good and bad)
  • Hadith of Jibrīl: Islam (5 pillars) → Īmān (6 pillars) → Iḥsān
  • Belief in Allah covers 4 things: His existence, Rubūbiyyah, Ulūhiyyah, Names & Attributes

Angels: key names

  • Jibrīl: revelation · Mīkāʾīl: rain and provision · Isrāfīl: blows the Trumpet
  • Angel of death · Munkar & Nakīr (questioning in the grave) · Kirāman Kātibīn (recorders)
  • They never disobey, need no food, and have wings

Messengers

  • Rasūl brings a new law; a Nabī follows an earlier law
  • Believe 4 things: they were truly sent; believe in the ones named; accept authentic reports about them; follow the law of the final Prophet
  • Tasks include conveying the message, calling to Tawḥīd, giving good news and warnings, and being a role model

Four rules (al-Qawāʿid al-Arbaʿ)

  • 1. The Makkan polytheists accepted Rubūbiyyah, but it did not make them Muslim
  • 2. They said, "We only seek nearness and intercession"
  • 3. They worshipped different things (angels, prophets, the righteous, trees, stones), and the Prophet ﷺ made no distinction
  • 4. Later polytheists are worse: earlier ones were sincere to Allah in hardship

Fortune-tellers & graves

  • Asking a fortune-teller: prayer not accepted for 40 nights. Believing them is disbelief in what was revealed
  • Visiting graves is allowed: to remember death, greet the dead and make duʿāʾ for them
  • Shirk: calling on the dead, sacrifice or ṭawāf at graves

FPEC 101 · Principles of Economics

1. Basics (L1–2)
  • Scarcity → choice → opportunity cost
  • PPC: points on the curve are efficient, inside is unemployment, outside is unattainable; bowed out = increasing opportunity cost
  • Systems: command vs market; circular flow (households ↔ firms)
2. Demand & Supply (L3–4)
  • Law of demand: P↑ → Qd↓; law of supply: P↑ → Qs↑
  • Own price = movement along the curve; other factors = a shift
  • Equilibrium at D = S; surplus above, shortage below
Markets & FirmsMR = MC
3. Elasticity (L4–5)
  • PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP; above 1 is elastic
  • Total revenue test: elastic demand means P↓ raises TR
  • Income elasticity: positive = normal good, negative = inferior good
  • Cross elasticity: positive = substitutes, negative = complements
4. Costs (L6–7)
  • TC = TFC + TVC; ATC = TC/Q; MC = ΔTC/ΔQ
  • Diminishing marginal returns in the short run
  • MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minimum
  • Long run: economies of scale, then constant returns, then diseconomies
5. Market structures (L8–11)
  • Perfect competition: P = MR, earns normal profit in the long run
  • Monopoly: P > MR, P > MC, deadweight loss
  • Monopolistic competition: differentiated products, excess capacity
  • Oligopoly: few firms, interdependent, game theory
6. Policy (L12–15)
  • Antitrust and regulation of natural monopoly
  • Wages: MRP = MRC; monopsony
  • Lorenz curve, Gini coefficient, poverty
  • Market failure: externalities, public goods; government failure

Revenue & profit formulas

  • TR = P × Q · AR = TR/Q = P · MR = ΔTR/ΔQ
  • π = TR − TC; maximise profit where MR = MC with MC rising
  • Shut down in the short run if P < minimum AVC
  • Economic profit = TR − (explicit + implicit costs)

Demand and supply shifters

  • Demand: tastes, number of buyers, income, prices of related goods, expectations
  • Supply: input prices, technology, taxes and subsidies, prices of other goods, expectations, number of sellers
  • Both curves shift: the change in either P or Q is indeterminate

Elasticity: quick rules

  • Elastic demand: many substitutes, luxuries, large share of the budget, long run
  • Unit elastic (PED = 1): TR is at its maximum
  • Supply is more elastic in the long run
  • Midpoint formula: ΔQ/[(Q1+Q2)/2] ÷ ΔP/[(P1+P2)/2]

Monopoly in detail

  • Barriers to entry: economies of scale, patents, licences, control of resources
  • Linear demand: the MR curve has twice the slope of demand
  • A monopoly operates only on the elastic part of demand
  • Price discrimination needs market power, segmented buyers, and no resale

Market failure

  • Negative externality (pollution): too much is produced → tax
  • Positive externality (education): too little → subsidy
  • Public goods are non-rival and non-excludable, so free riders appear
  • Gini coefficient: 0 = equal, 1 = completely unequal

BIBS 101 · Introduction to Business

1. Role of Business (M1)
  • Business: provides goods and services for profit
  • Profit = revenue − expenses; risk brings reward
  • Stakeholders: owners, employees, customers, suppliers, community, government
  • Factors of production: land, labour, capital, entrepreneurship, knowledge
2. Economic Environment (M2)
  • Micro vs macro; demand and supply
  • Systems: capitalism, socialism, communism, mixed
  • Measures: GDP, unemployment, inflation (CPI)
  • Business cycle: expansion, peak, contraction, trough
  • Fiscal policy (tax and spending) vs monetary policy (money supply, interest rates)
Business& its environment
3. Global Environment (M3)
  • Exporting, importing, balance of trade and balance of payments
  • Entry modes: licensing, franchising, contract manufacturing, joint venture, FDI
  • Barriers: culture, law, tariffs, quotas, embargoes
  • Trade bodies: WTO, IMF, World Bank, regional blocs
4. Finance & Law (M4–5)
  • Money: medium of exchange, store of value, unit of account
  • Central bank, commercial banks, stock market
  • Law: contracts (offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, legality), torts, intellectual property, bankruptcy
5. Ethics & Ownership (M6–7)
  • Ethics: right vs wrong; CSR toward stakeholders
  • Ownership: sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, cooperative
  • Mergers: horizontal, vertical, conglomerate
6. Entrepreneurship & Management (M8–9)
  • Entrepreneur: takes risk to start a business; small business and the business plan
  • Functions: planning, organising, leading, controlling
  • Management skills: technical, human, conceptual
  • SWOT analysis; mission, goals, objectives

Forms of ownership

FormAdvantagesDisadvantages
Sole proprietoreasy to start, owner keeps profit, full controlunlimited liability, limited funds

Global trade terms

  • Comparative advantage: produce what you give up least to make
  • Absolute advantage: produce more efficiently than others
  • Trade surplus = exports > imports; deficit = the reverse
  • Dumping: selling abroad below cost
  • Protectionism: tariffs, quotas, embargoes

Economic indicators

  • GDP = total value of final goods and services produced in a country
  • Inflation = general rise in prices; deflation = general fall
  • Unemployment types: frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal
  • Recession = two or more quarters of falling GDP

Ethics & CSR

  • Levels: individual → managerial → organisational
  • CSR toward customers, investors, employees, society and the environment
  • Approaches: compliance-based vs integrity-based codes
  • Whistleblower: reports wrongdoing inside an organisation

Management

  • Planning: strategic → tactical → operational → contingency
  • Leadership styles: autocratic, participative, laissez-faire
  • Levels: top, middle, supervisory
  • SWOT = Strengths and Weaknesses (internal), Opportunities and Threats (external)

Finance basics

  • Money supply: M1 (cash and checking accounts), M2 (M1 plus savings)
  • Central bank tools: reserve requirement, discount rate, open market operations
  • Stocks = ownership; bonds = debt

ENES 101 · English Language Studies

1. Learning (M1–2)
  • Learning styles: auditory, visual, kinesthetic (most people are a mix)
  • Critical thinking, creative thinking, meta-cognition
  • Vocabulary: context clues, word parts (prefix, root, suffix), dictionary, word lists
2. Reading (M3–4)
  • Skimming: for the main idea · Scanning: for a specific fact
  • SQ3R: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review
  • Annotating: underline, write in margins, use symbols, summarise
Academic Skillsread → note → write
3. Note Taking (M5–8)
  • Methods: Cornell, outline, mind map, charting
  • Use abbreviations and symbols; record key words, not every word
  • Mind map: central idea, branches, key words, colours
  • Outline: I. → A. → 1. → a.
4. Arguments & Plagiarism (M9–10)
  • Argument = claim + reasons + evidence
  • Fact vs opinion; watch for logical fallacies
  • Plagiarism = using another person's words or ideas without credit
  • Avoid it by quoting, paraphrasing, summarising and citing
5. Writing Process (M11–13)
  • Prewriting → outlining → drafting → revising → editing
  • Prewriting methods: listing, freewriting, clustering, questioning
  • For an assignment: analyse the question, gather ideas, narrow the topic
6. Draft & Revise (M14–15)
  • Thesis = topic + controlling idea (the whole essay)
  • Topic sentence = topic + controlling idea (one paragraph)
  • Revise content and organisation first, then edit grammar, spelling and punctuation

Paragraph & essay structure

  • Paragraph: topic sentence → supporting sentences (examples, facts) → concluding sentence
  • Essay: introduction (hook, background, thesis) → body paragraphs → conclusion (restate the thesis, summarise, give a final thought)
  • Unity: one idea per paragraph · Coherence: transitions and logical order

Note-taking tips

  • Before: preview the topic · During: key words, headings, examples · After: review within 24 hours
  • Cornell format: cue column, notes column, summary at the bottom
  • Presentations: note the main points, not every word on the slides

Thesis checklist

  • One sentence giving a clear position
  • Not a fact, a question, or too broad
  • Usually the last sentence of the introduction
  • Example: "Social media improves communication but harms students' time management."

APA 7 essentials

  • Times New Roman 12, double spacing, 1-inch margins, 0.5-inch first-line indent
  • In-text citation: (Author, Year) or (Author, Year, p. #)
  • References on a new page, alphabetical order, hanging indent
  • Student title page: title, name, university, course, instructor, date

Revising vs editing

  • Revising = the big picture: ideas, order, unity, adding or cutting material
  • Editing = the small things: grammar, spelling, punctuation, word choice
  • Read aloud; check against the rubric
