The method for all four (from the AssignmentFAQs)

  1. Choose ONE topic ("OR" means one, not both). Pick the one where you understand the most modules.
  2. Underline the keywords in the question. Every "you should…" bullet is a section the marker will look for (FAQ 33: identify keywords, make an outline).
  3. Read and take notes in your own words, with the source and page number beside each note. Then close the books and write from your notes (IOU policy: "write your assignment from your notes, not the text").
  4. Skeleton: Introduction (about the topic, never "This assignment is for…", FAQ 35) → Body (several paragraphs, FAQ 44) → Conclusion. No abstract for 1000–1200 words (FAQ 37).
  5. Cite while you write: an in-text citation for every idea you took from somewhere, including paraphrases (FAQ 7). Qur'an: (Surah:Verse) and the translation goes in the reference list (FAQ 24). Hadith: (Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol., Book, No.) in the text and the translator in the reference list (FAQ 27).
  6. References: 5–10 is normal (FAQ 10); the minimum is 3 (FPEC, BIBS, ENES). Use books and journals, not random websites, YouTube or Wikipedia.
  7. Similarity below 20%. Quote little and paraphrase a lot.
  8. Format: Word file (not PDF), Times New Roman 12, double spacing, black text. The first page has Name, ID, Course, Assignment Question and the signed declaration. File name: ISGS101_FirstName. Press Submit, not just upload.

Topic 1: paragraph plan (~1,150 words)

  1. Introduction (~200, 20%): what a market is; the price mechanism; why firms care about revenue and profit; a preview of the sections; your thesis (e.g. that all firms use the same MR = MC rule, but market power changes the price and output that result).
  2. Demand (~150): definition (willing + able + price + time), the law of demand and its reasons (income and substitution effects, diminishing marginal utility), a demand schedule → Figure 1 (downward curve), and movement along the curve vs a shift (with determinants).
  3. Supply (~120): definition, the law of supply, determinants, and Figure 2.
  4. Equilibrium (~120): Qd = Qs; what happens with a surplus or a shortage; Figure 3 with your own numbers; one Bangladesh example (e.g. onion prices before Eid).
  5. TR, AR and MR (~150): TR = P × Q, MR = ΔTR/ΔQ. Make a small table of your own (Q, P, TR, MR) for (a) a price taker (P constant → MR = P) and (b) a firm with a downward demand curve (MR falls faster than P).
  6. Profit maximisation in perfect competition (~150): profit is maximised where MR = MC (with MC rising); P = MR = AR; short-run profit or loss and the shutdown rule (P < min AVC); long run: entry and exit until P = min ATC (zero economic profit). Figure 4.
  7. Imperfect competition (~170): monopoly (price maker, MR below D, set Q at MR = MC then read P from the demand curve, P > MC, profit can persist); briefly monopolistic competition (zero long-run profit, excess capacity) and oligopoly (interdependence, kinked demand). Figure 5, plus a small comparison table.
  8. Conclusion / opinion (~100): summary + your view (e.g. why regulators watch monopolies such as utilities, Lecture 12).

Graphs: draw them yourself (Word shapes, Excel or a neat hand drawing photographed). Label the axes, curves and equilibrium, and number them Figure 1, 2… with a short caption. Your own numbers and graphs also keep your similarity score low.

Topic 2: paragraph plan (if you prefer it)
  1. Introduction (~200): what elasticity measures and why firms and government care; thesis.
  2. PED (~250): formula %ΔQd/%ΔP and the midpoint formula; a worked example with your own numbers; elastic, inelastic, unit, perfectly elastic and perfectly inelastic, with a graph of each; determinants.
  3. PED and total revenue (~120): the total-revenue test with a small table.
  4. Income elasticity (~100) and cross elasticity (~100): formulas, signs (normal/inferior; substitutes/complements) and an example each.
  5. Price elasticity of supply (~100): formula; immediate, short run, long run.
  6. Price discrimination (~180): definition; conditions (market power, separable markets, different PEDs, no resale); a higher price to the inelastic group; a two-market graph; Bangladesh examples (student fares, peak/off-peak tickets).
  7. Conclusion / opinion (~100).

Topic 1 (CSR): section plan following the 6 bullets (~1,150 words)

Write it as a report for investors, with headings that mirror the bullets:

  1. Introduction: company and why selected (~150): sector, size, country, one striking fact; why it is a good CSR case; a thesis answering the final question in advance (does CSR give it a competitive advantage?).
  2. CSR and business ethics (~170): define CSR (BIBS Module 6, cited from the textbook); link it to ethics (ethics = right/wrong standards; CSR = acting on them towards society). Carroll's pyramid: economic → legal → ethical → philanthropic.
  3. Three CSR initiatives, evaluated (~330, ~110 each): for each, what (name, year, size, from the report) → which pyramid level → evaluation (real impact or just marketing? any numbers?).
  4. Stakeholders (~170): direct (employees, customers, investors, suppliers) vs indirect (community, government, environment, NGOs, media). A small table works well: initiative × stakeholder × effect.
  5. One controversy or dilemma (~170): a real criticism from a reliable news source; what the company did; your judgement of whether the response was effective, and why.
  6. Conclusion: competitive advantage? (~150): your opinion with evidence (brand trust, customer loyalty, talent, investor ESG interest; Porter & Kramer's "shared value"), plus a recommendation to the investors.
Topic 2 (Globalisation): section plan
  1. Introduction: company, countries and regions (~150).
  2. Define globalisation; why it matters to modern business (~150).
  3. Three opportunities and three challenges for this company (~300), linked to the global forces from BIBS Module 3: sociocultural, economic/financial (exchange rates), legal/regulatory (tariffs, quotas), physical/environmental.
  4. Four global strategies (~280), each with a real example: exporting, licensing, franchising, contract manufacturing, joint venture/strategic alliance, foreign direct investment (subsidiary). Pick four.
  5. Evaluate one recent decision (an expansion, partnership, launch or marketing move): was it an effective response to globalisation? (~170)
  6. Conclusion: how understanding globalisation keeps firms competitive (~100).

ENES 101: an essay with prewriting and outline

Topic 1: The role of honesty and integrity in building a strong Muslim character.
Topic 2: The influence of social media on university students' academic and social lives.

One Word document with: Prewriting/brainstorm (15 marks) + Detailed outline (15) + Final essay (45) + Grammar (10) + APA format (10) + APA referencing (5). Final copy 1000–1200 words (only the final copy is counted); APA 7th edition; 3–5 credible references.

Recommendation: Topic 2 (social media). It is easy to brainstorm from your own life, there is plenty of academic research, and positive vs negative effects give you a natural structure. Topic 1 is also fine if you like Islamic evidence; it overlaps with ISGS.

Part 1: Prewriting (15 marks): this is your messy rough work

The sheet says it "does not need to be perfect". It must look like real thinking, so do it yourself, by hand or in Word. Use two of the formats from ENES Modules 12–13:

  • Questions (5W1H): Who uses social media most? What platforms? When (late at night)? Why do students use it? How does it affect studying, friendships and time?
  • Brainstorm list (words and phrases only): group study on Messenger, YouTube tutorials, distraction, notifications, sleep, FOMO, comparing lives, staying in touch with family, cyberbullying, screen time…
  • Mind map (Tony Buzan, Module 7): the topic in the centre; branches = learning, communication, relationships, time; plus/minus on each.

Then circle your 3 strongest ideas. These become your body paragraphs.

Part 2: Detailed outline (15 marks): the format is marked

Follow Module 8's rules: I, II, III → A, B → 1, 2 → a, b; no I without II, no A without B; capitalise the first word only; short phrases (or full sentences if you make a sentence outline, but be consistent); parallel structure. The thesis goes in I.

I. Introduction
   A. Hook / background
   B. Thesis: (your one-sentence position: topic + opinion)
II. Effects on learning
   A. Positive
      1. …
      2. …
   B. Negative
      1. …
      2. …
III. Effects on communication and relationships
   A. …
   B. …
IV. Effects on use of time
   A. …
   B. …
V. Conclusion
   A. Restate thesis
   B. Recommendation

Use this as the shape only; the points must come from your own brainstorm. Each body section should have both a positive and a negative side, because the question says "consider both".

Part 3: Final essay (~1,100 words)

  1. Introduction (~150): hook (a statistic or situation), background, thesis as the last sentence (Module 14: topic + opinion, not a fact or an announcement).
  2. Body 1: learning (~250): topic sentence → positives (online resources, group chats) → negatives (distraction, multitasking) → evidence (e.g. Kirschner & Karpinski, 2010; Junco, 2012) → link to thesis.
  3. Body 2: communication and relationships (~250): staying connected vs shallow contact, comparison, cyberbullying; evidence.
  4. Body 3: use of time (~250): time-wasting, sleep, addictive design vs planning tools; evidence.
  5. Conclusion (~150): restate the thesis in new words, summarise, and give a practical recommendation (balance, app limits). No new information.

Then revise (content) and edit (grammar, spelling), as in Modules 11 and 15: let it sit for a few days, read it aloud, and check every paragraph has a topic sentence.

If you choose Topic 1 (honesty & integrity): content ideas and evidence
  • Definitions: honesty (sidq) = truth in speech and action; integrity = being the same in private and public, keeping trusts (amanah).
  • Qur'an: 9:119 ("be with the truthful"), 33:70–71 (speak words of appropriate justice), 4:58 (render trusts to whom they are due).
  • Hadith: truthfulness leads to righteousness and Paradise (Sahih al-Bukhari 6094; Sahih Muslim 2607); the signs of a hypocrite: lies when he speaks, breaks promises, betrays trust (Sahih al-Bukhari 33); "He who deceives us is not one of us" (Sahih Muslim), quoted on your own assignment sheet!
  • Example: the Prophet ﷺ was known as al-Amin even before prophethood.
  • Body structure: relationships (trust in family and friendships) · decisions (business, exams, no cheating) · everyday behaviour (online honesty, keeping promises).
