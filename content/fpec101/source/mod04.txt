=== Slide 9
- “Elasticity is the responsiveness of quantity to a change in price”
- In simple terms, it is the extent to which quantity changes with a change in price.  It may also be said that it is a proportionate change in quantity due to a change in price.  It is denoted by ‘E’
- ELASTICITY
- PED – Price Elasticity of Demand
- PES – Price Elasticity of Supply
[pics=0 charts=0 tables=0]
=== Slide 10
- ELASTICITY
- If a small change in price, produce a bigger change in demand  demand is elastic.
- If a large change in price, produce a small change in demand  demand is inelastic.

=== Slide 18
- Elasticity of Demand
- Change in price brings about absolutely no change in quantity demanded.
- This means that a change in price brings about an infinite change in quantity.  Even a small change in price will create a new demand curve and quantity demanded at the previous price would be zero
- This means that a change in price brings about an equal or proportionate change in quantity demanded
[NOTES] The unit elastic demand curve is a good one to use to emphasize that elasticity and slope are not equal. Have the students calculate the elasticity of supply on two linear demand curves that passes through the origin, one with a slope of 0.5 and the other with a slope of 2. They’ll get the message.

=== Slide 19
- Determinants of Elasticity of demand
- Availability of close substitutesIf substitutes are available, demand is elastic because the consumer can shift to the other product
- Necessity or luxury The demand for necessities is in elastic example fuel, salt.  Demand for luxuries is elastic example going on a vacation.
- Income portion spent on commodityIf large, demand is elasticIf small, demand is in elastic as it does not form a major portion of income
- Habit forming goods
- Those which are addictive have inelastic demand
- Time period
- If long, or purchasing can be postponed there is a chance to look for substitute, so 	demand is elastic and vice Versa.
